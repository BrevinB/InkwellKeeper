## Reddit r/Lorcana — collector tip post

**Title:** Foils and normals are two different markets — a tracking habit that saves collectors money

**Body:**
I track daily prices across every Lorcana chase card (483 printings), and one thing the data keeps reinforcing: a foil is not "the normal, but shinier." It's a separate printing trading in a separate, much thinner market. A few practical takeaways:

1. **Foil prices move slower and gappier.** Way fewer foil copies trade hands, so foil "market price" can sit stale for weeks, then jump on a single sale. If you're valuing a binder using normal prices × some multiplier, you're guessing.

2. **The foil premium isn't constant — and the direction it moves tells you why a card is wanted.** On competitive staples, demand is for the cheapest playable copy, so the *normal* price climbs toward the foil and the premium compresses. On collector-driven cards, the premium widens instead. Watching which way the gap moves is a decent tell for whether a card's price is meta-driven (fragile — rotation/bans can kill it) or collector-driven (stickier).

3. **Log your pulls as the printing you actually pulled.** Sounds obvious, but if your tracking method lumps foils and normals together, your collection value is wrong in both directions — and when it's time to trade, foil comps are what matter for foils, not normal price plus vibes.

4. **When selling or trading a foil, pull foil comps specifically.** Recent *sold* foil listings, not the lowest active one — in a thin market the lowest active listing is often either a fantasy or a fire sale.

One market note from this week's data: it was a quietly red week across the board — the credible movers were all losers (The Madrigal Family - Every Generation $86 → $76, Aladdin - Barreling Through $9.47 → $7.72), so buyers waiting on singles are still being rewarded.
