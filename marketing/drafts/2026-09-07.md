## Reddit r/Lorcana — collector tip post

**Title:** The set completion trap: why the last 15% of a set costs as much as the first 85% — and how to actually beat it

**Body:**

I've been tracking Lorcana prices across 483 chase cards for a while (I build a collection app), and I've watched a lot of collectors hit the same wall. Here's the pattern and the fix.

**Why the last cards are always the expensive ones**

Most collectors buy what excites them first — the enchanteds, the rares they recognize from tournaments or content. What that leaves behind isn't commons. It's the pricey mid-tier singles nobody talks about online: the $25 support card, the $40 rarely-featured rare. By the time you realize they're the last ones you need, you've already spent your budget on the flashy stuff.

**Build a full gap list early**

Before you buy anything else, list every card you need, sorted by price descending. This tells you the truth: you might have 4 expensive singles left and 60 cheap ones. That changes what you buy first.

**Buy expensive singles early; grind cheap ones late**

A $0.50 common is $0.50 now and in six months. A $30 rare might be $15 in six months — or $60. Front-load the expensive cards while prices are still discoverable. The cheap filler will wait for you.

**Set completion and collection value are different goals**

If you care about completing a set, the cheap-card grind is the endgame and you have to embrace it. If you care about portfolio value, cheap cards will never meaningfully move your number — ignore them. Be honest with yourself about which goal you're actually chasing, or you'll optimize for the wrong one.

**Watch the market, not the hype**

By the time something spikes on social media, the deal is gone. The actionable signal is a card sitting at its 30-day floor that you happen to need — buy it before the content arrives.
