## Reddit r/Lorcana — collector tip post

**Title:** How to tell if a Lorcana price spike is real before you buy (or sell) into it

**Body:**

I track daily prices on about 490 chase cards (I build a collection app), and every week my data shows a handful of cards "up 1,000%+." Almost none of them are real. Here's the checklist I use before I believe a spike:

**1. Look at the starting price, not the percentage.**
A card going from $1 to $20 is +1,900%. That's one odd listing or one sale, not a market. Big percentages on sub-$3 cards are the least trustworthy numbers in the hobby.

**2. Check sold listings, not asking prices.**
One seller listing a common at $25 doesn't make it a $25 card. Filter for completed sales. If there are only one or two at the new price, it isn't a move yet.

**3. Ask "why this week?"**
Real spikes usually have a cause: a tournament result, a ban or rotation change, a reprint announcement, a creator feature. No cause usually means no spike.

**4. Watch for repeat offenders.**
If the same card "spikes" week after week and never holds, that's a data artifact. In my own data, the same handful of First Chapter and Into the Inklands cards (a few Mickeys, Gantu, Maleficent - Monstrous Dragon, Te Kā) keep showing up with 10x "gains" from sub-$4 bases. I don't trust any of them, and you shouldn't either.

**5. Declines on cheap cards are usually just settling.**
Right now a handful of Attack of the Vine! singles in the $2–3 range are down roughly 17–29% on the week, which is under a dollar each. About 10 weeks after a set launches, that's normal supply catching up, not a crash.

**The rule of thumb:** trust dollar moves on cards with steady sales. Be skeptical of percentage moves on cards that barely trade.

(I build Ink Well Keeper, a free iOS app with daily price history on every card. Happy to answer questions about how I track this in the comments.)
