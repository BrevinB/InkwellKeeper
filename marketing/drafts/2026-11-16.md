## Reddit r/Lorcana: collector tip (text post)

**Title:** PSA: how to tell a real price spike from a data glitch before you buy or sell

> I track daily prices on a few hundred Lorcana chase cards. For a month, my data has insisted that a handful of cheap First Chapter and Into the Inklands cards went up 1,500–3,000% every week. They haven't. It's a data problem I'm still untangling. It's also a good reminder that price sites and trackers get things wrong too, so here's the checklist I use now before I trust a "spike."
>
> **1. Check recent sold listings, not the listed price.**
> A market price is an estimate. A handful of actual sales over the last week or two tells you what people really paid.
>
> **2. Look at how many sales there are.**
> One or two sales on a low-volume card can swing the market price wildly. If a card "jumped" on a single sale, wait for a second one.
>
> **3. Make sure you're comparing the same printing.**
> Normal, foil, Enchanted, and promo versions of the same card can have very different prices. A lot of "this card 10x'd overnight" moments are really one version's price getting attached to another.
>
> **4. Ask whether there's a reason.**
> A new deck winning a big event, a reprint announcement, or a card getting banned are real reasons. "Nothing happened and it's up 3,000%" usually means something's wrong with the data.
>
> **5. Be suspicious of spikes that repeat.**
> If a card shows the same huge jump week after week from the same starting price, it's not a trend. It's a glitch.
>
> For contrast, here's what a believable move looks like: Hyperia City's Remember Me went from about $141 to $124 in my data this week. That's a modest drop on a newer set that's still settling, in line with other Hyperia City chase cards since launch. No drama, and it's consistent with the trend.
>
> What's the wildest "price spike" you've seen that turned out to be nothing?
>
