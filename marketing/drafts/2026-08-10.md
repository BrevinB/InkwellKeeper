## Reddit r/Lorcana — collector tip post

**Title:** How to tell a real Lorcana price spike from a thin-market blip (with this week's examples)

**Body:**
I track daily prices across every Lorcana chase card (483 printings), and this week my own data handed me a perfect case study in why you should never trust a big % move at face value.

My tracker's top "gainer" this week: Maleficent - Monstrous Dragon (The First Chapter), $1.88 → $35.62, a +1,795% move. Also "spiking": Te Kā - Heartless (+1,311%) and Mickey Mouse - Trumpeter (+937%). Three-year-old cards don't 10x in a week without a reason, and there is no reason — no new deck, no reprint news, nothing. These are almost certainly one or two outlier sales (or a data correction) in a market with very few listings.

The three checks I run before believing any spike, mine or anyone else's:

1. **Sales volume.** If only a couple of copies traded, one ambitious listing or one impatient buyer *is* the market that week.
2. **Market price vs lowest listing.** When "market price" says $35 but you can still buy the card for $3, the $35 is noise.
3. **Is there a story?** Real moves have causes — a deck doing well, rotation talk, a ban, supply news. A spike with no story usually isn't demand.

What the *credible* side of my data shows this week: Wilds Unknown chase cards cooling as more product gets opened — You've Got a Friend in Me $440 → $382 (-13%), Buzz Lightyear - Space Ranger $10.54 → $8.88, Woody - Waiting for a Friend $11.11 → $9.13. And Attack of the Vine's top card, Rapunzel & Flynn Rider - Unlikely Pair, continued last week's slide, $220 → $187. Slow, broad, post-launch drift across a whole set — that's what real movement usually looks like. Boring, consistent, explainable.

Nothing here is financial advice; mostly it's a plea to be suspicious of screenshots with huge green percentages on them.
